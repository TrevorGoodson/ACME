#### Sets
$\mathbb N$ is the set of all natural numbers ($0$ is not included)
$\mathbb Z$ is the set of all integers
$\mathbb R$ is the set of all real numbers
$\mathbb C$ is the set of all complex numbers
$\mathbb F$ is a "field"--it's either $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$. If a proof uses this, it can work for either one. Yes, it's a little silly since $\mathbb R \subset \mathbb C$ and you can always just replace $\mathbb F$ with $\mathbb C$.
$\mathbb M_{m \times n}(\mathbb F)$ is the set of all matrixes that have $m$ rows and $n$ columns, where each element is in $\mathbb F$.
$\mathscr B(X, Y)$ is the set of all [[Bounded Linear Transformation]]s from $X$ to $Y$.

#### Set Operations
$A \subseteq B$ means $A$ is a subset of or equal to $B$
$\subset$ is equivalent to $\subseteq$. Since this notation isn't as clear as $\subseteq$, this is typically used when equality isn't worth considering explicitly
$\subsetneq$ is used when equality is explicitly not allowed

#### Other Operations
$\| \cdot \|$ is the [[Norm]]
$| x |$ is the absolute value of $x$ (if $x$ is a scalar) or it is the [[Determinant]] of $x$ (if $x$ is a matrix)
$\lambda (A)$ is the [[Measure]] of $A$ (where $A$ is a set).
$(\cdot)^T$ is the [[Transpose]]
$(\cdot)^H$ is the [[Hermitian]]
$\bar X$ can either mean the [[Closed|closure]], [[Complex Conjugate]], or just used to make a new symbol (probably to indicate that $\bar X$ is linked to $X$, but are different). This one is heavily context dependent.
$X \times Y$ is the [[Cartesian Product]] of $X$ and $Y$ (where $X$ and $Y$ are sets)
